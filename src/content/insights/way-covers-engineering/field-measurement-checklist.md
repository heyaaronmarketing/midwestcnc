A damaged way cover looks minor until the guideway beneath starts pitting. At that point, the cheapest part on the machine has caused the most costly repair. Getting a new cover made requires real dimensions—not rough guesses. This checklist exists because we have seen too many covers arrive at the dock 8mm off on mounting holes. The goal is one site visit, one session, all data captured, quote returned, cover made, machine back in cut.

## The 11-Point Checklist

This list works for telescoping steel covers, bellows, roll-ups, and hybrids. Not every point applies to every style. But capturing all eleven means the fabricator has what is needed regardless of type.

1. **Overall travel length.** Measure the axis stroke with the cover removed or fully extended. On a Mazak VTC-200, the X-axis travel is 2,000mm. The cover must handle that stroke plus 20-50mm of overtravel.

2. **Compressed stack height.** When the axis compresses the cover fully, measure the total stack height. A 7-section cover for an [Okuma CNC way cover](/way-covers/okuma-cnc-way-covers/) might compress to 90mm. If the machine has only 85mm of space at that position, the cover will bind.

3. **Extended length.** Measure compressed and extended states at each stroke extreme. Extended length is not simply travel plus stack height. It accounts for overlap in telescoping covers or pleat geometry in bellows.

4. **Cover width.** Measure at the mounting surface, not the outer face. A slant-bed cover may have different top and bottom widths. Record both.

5. **Cover height (profile).** The vertical face dimension. On box-way machines this is often 50-150mm. On slant beds it can exceed 200mm.

6. **Mounting hole pattern.** Document hole count, diameter, spacing, and distance from edges. Photograph the mounting hardware from both ends. This detail alone clears most quote confusion.

7. **Mounting style.** Covers mount by screws, clips, slides, or combos. Some older Haas machines use tab-and-slot designs. The [Way Covers for Haas Classic Control Era Machines](/way-covers/haas-cnc-way-covers/haas-classic-control/) page shows variations.

8. **Wiper pocket dimensions.** If the cover meets a wiper or scraper, measure the pocket. Wipers are often 3-5mm thick. The cover's leading edge must clear them.

9. **End cap or scraper bar config.** Some covers end in a rigid cap; others in a flexible lip. Document what exists and measure its profile.

10. **Material notes.** Record if the cover is steel, stainless, aluminum, fabric, or hybrid. Note any coating color or texture. If it is steel versus fabric bellows, that choice affects price and lead time.

11. **Environment notes.** Document the chip environment. Record coolant type. These factors guide material selection. The choice between coated steel and stainless often hinges on coolant chemistry.

This 11-point checklist works for X, Y, and Z axes. It also applies to rotary covers and panels. The [Z-Axis Covers: Gravity, Drag, and Design Choices](/insights/way-covers-engineering/z-axis-cover-design/) article covers added concerns for vertical covers.

## What to Photograph for a Remote Quote

Photos reduce ambiguity that numbers alone cannot capture. We have seen forms filled out correctly, yet the cover had a compound curve that only showed up when the new part did not fit.

Photograph the mounting hardware from both sides of each section. A 6-section cover means 12 photos of mount points. This sounds like a lot. But hole placement often varies between inner and outer sections.

Take a full-length shot at both extremes of travel. One photo with the axis compressed, one at full extension. These confirm stroke and show any sag in the old cover.

Photograph damage in detail. Tears, creases, and dents tell us if the frame is bent. A bent frame needs full replacement. A damaged skin on a sound frame may allow a re-skin.

Photograph the guideway where the cover mounts. Show the rails, wipers, end stops. If debris is packed under the cover, capture that too. It documents contamination severity.

Capture any labels or part numbers. OEM numbers speed matching. Even partial numbers help. A Mazak cover might show "COVER ASSY X 1234567." That number, shot clearly, lets us cross-check [Mazak CNC Way Covers](/way-covers/mazak-cnc-way-covers/) records.

Include a scale reference in at least one photo. A 6-inch rule or machinist's scale in the frame lets us sanity-check dimensions.

A complete package is 12-15 photos plus the filled form. We usually return a quote in 48 hours on complete sets. Missing details push that to 5-7 days.

## Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

The most frequent error is measuring at mid-stroke. If you read compressed height while the axis is at center travel, you get a number that is neither end state. The new cover then binds at one extreme.

Measuring outer width instead of mount-surface width is another trap. A cover flares outward from section to section. The outer section may be 350mm wide. The inner mount surface may be 310mm. Using 350mm yields a cover that cannot attach.

Assuming symmetry trips up many jobs. A left-side table cover is not always a mirror of the right. Makers sometimes offset holes for clearance. Measure each cover on its own.

Using worn or crashed covers as direct templates adds error. A cover hit by a forklift has deformation. Measure the machine's true geometry. Check guideway length, stack space, and hole positions on the machine frame. ## Tools for Field Measurement

Accurate work does not need costly gear. A basic kit includes:

- **Steel tape, 25 ft / 8m.** Covers often exceed 2 meters extended.
- **Digital calipers, 300mm.** For holes, stack heights, and profiles.
- **Straightedge, 1 meter.** To detect sag, bow, or twist.
- **Angle finder.** Needed for slant beds and compound covers.
- **Notepad or printed form.** Some shops write on-site and photo the form.
- **Smartphone or camera.** For the 12-15 photos above.

Avoid cloth tapes. They stretch and add error on long covers. Avoid laser measurers for stack heights. They work well at room scale but lack precision in the 50-200mm range.

## How This Data Becomes a Quote

When a complete package arrives, we check it against two sources. First, OEM specs where available. Second, our internal log of covers built for that model. If your extended length reads 1,850mm and the OEM spec says 1,900mm, we call. That 50mm gap might be a slip, a non-standard travel config, or a sign the old cover was trimmed.

Material choice flows from your environment notes. A shop cutting aluminum with water-based coolant often uses standard rolled steel covers with a treatment. A shop cutting titanium with straight oil may need stainless or coated steel. The cost gap can be 15-30%.

Lead time depends on complexity, material stock, and backlog. The [Cover Replacement Lead Time: What Drives It](/insights/way-covers-engineering/cover-replacement-lead-time/) article breaks down these factors. A standard cover for a common model may ship in 2-3 weeks. A custom bellows with exotic material and compound curves can stretch to 6-8 weeks.
